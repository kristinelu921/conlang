# Overnight communication investigation

Status: `completed`

## Current evidence

The baseline model sends four continuous hidden vectors from each sender. Its receiver encoder mixes the eight vectors with the question, and a decoder predicts one answer token plus EOS. The bypass arms mask the receiver's direct question input and expose the question only to the senders. The latest unsupervised arm instead keeps senders question-blind and uses the receiver question only as attention queries over message keys and values. Each source world contains eight linked chains and eight questions; every sender passage contains one hop from every chain, while the complementary hop is in the other passage.

The exact 256-example validation prefix has a 0.03515625 empirical baseline when the answer is guessed from only the relation domain inferred from the question. A full-data question-template lookup reaches 0.0703125. The direct-question full-data runs' 0.0390625--0.046875 range is therefore consistent with a question-only shortcut. The direct causal baseline confirms it: intact, zero, wrong-world, swapped, agent-1-only, and agent-2-only messages all yield the same 10 correct answers. Zeroing messages improves mean joint log probability by 0.0022974. Final sender-path gradient norms are 0.00452 and 0.00540 while the receiver path is 1.13643, about 210--251 times larger.

## Ranked hypotheses and tests

1. **Question-only shortcut dominates optimization — confirmed for the baseline.** The receiver learns the answer prior for each relation type while assigning no measurable causal weight to the messages. Intact, zero, and wrong-world messages produce identical exact predictions and nearly identical likelihoods.
2. **Four query slots are too narrow for eight facts per sender — ruled out as the primary blocker.** Doubling to eight vectors leaves exact predictions unchanged across all message interventions and makes the sender/receiver gradient imbalance larger.
3. **Two layers are too shallow for character parsing, fact selection, and a two-hop join — ruled out as the primary blocker.** Depth 4 halves throughput but keeps intact messages no better than zero or wrong-world messages.
4. **Capacity did not become a useful stacked improvement after communication was established.** Hidden 512 alone had already failed to move beyond the shortcut regime. Retesting width 256 on the bridge-supervised communicating architecture preserved passage use, but reduced answer accuracy and the causal passage gap while lowering throughput.
5. **Tying and role gates are secondary and did not improve the best communicating architecture.** Earlier matched small-prefix pairs and full-data runs showed no robust benefit. The controlled hidden-256 bridge-supervised comparison then found that tying modestly improved raw accuracy and efficiency while weakening the passage-specific causal advantage.
6. **Question conditioning alone shortens the path but does not repair credit assignment — ruled out as a sufficient intervention.** Target-question/wrong-passages matches intact exact accuracy and improves likelihood, while sender gradients become even smaller relative to the receiver.
7. **Channel capacity matters only after the shortcut is blocked — ruled out as the private-fact blocker.** Message length 8 makes the active channel's question content explicit: wrong-question re-encoding loses 7/256 exact answers and 6.28 nats. Wrong passages still match intact exact accuracy and slightly improve likelihood, so extra slots do not create private-fact transport.
8. **The tested synchronous second exchange supplies the missing join path — ruled out as a sufficient architectural fix.** The exchange gives either sender the other sender's first-round representation, yet wrong passages preserve all 10 exact answers and slightly improve likelihood. This interaction design remains passage-flat.
9. **The end-to-end answer objective supplies too little bridge-selection signal — strongly supported as a material blocker.** Explicit bridge supervision lifts bridge accuracy to 32/256 from 6/256 under either wrong questions or wrong passages. Intact answers beat wrong passages by 5/256 and 0.0431 nats, creating the first beneficial private-passage gap. This establishes that direct bridge credit repairs an important failure mode in this setup, not that it is the unique remaining cause.
10. **Coupled transformer/message width 256 is not a useful stacked improvement in this arm.** Private-passage use persists, and bridge accuracy rises slightly from 32/256 to 34/256, but intact answer accuracy falls from 17/256 to 14/256, the matched passage gap narrows from 5/256 to 3/256, and throughput drops 26.81%. Do not keep doubled width as the default from this single-seed result.
11. **Weight sharing is not a useful stacked improvement at hidden width 256.** Tying raises raw exact match by one answer and bridge accuracy by four, but the matched answer passage gap shrinks from 3/256 to 2/256 and from 0.0409 to 0.0255 nats. The bridge passage-control advantage is also 28 answers tied versus 29 untied. Tying improves median throughput by 2.42% and reduces checkpoint size, but does not strengthen passage-specific communication.
12. **Question-only queries over message keys and values do not repair unsupervised credit assignment.** Structurally removing question values from decoder memory raises the two absolute sender-path gradient medians by 1.60x and 1.79x, but the receiver remains about 330x larger. Intact and wrong-world messages both answer 3/256, wrong-world is marginally better in likelihood, and the final logged training batch collapses to one answer. The restriction is therefore not sufficient without direct bridge credit.
13. **Insufficient aggregate sender optimizer motion is ruled out as the sole blocker.** A 4x post-Adam sender-private multiplier moves the middle-training loss transition from about step 980 to step 830, gives sender parameters 2.36x the receiver's median update-to-weight ratio over the last 50 logged steps, and increases final sender weight norm 1.31% from initialization rather than 0.04%. Nevertheless, wrong-world remains tied at 4/256 and slightly better in likelihood, zero messages remain substantially better in likelihood, and the final logged batch emits one answer for all examples. The phase transition is real, but it is not passage-specific communication.
14. **Raw sender-gradient starvation is ruled out more strongly, not merely mitigated.** On 1M training questions over 10,000 steps, the shared hidden-192/depth-4/FFN-768 sender with an average-passage residual reduces final-50 receiver-to-sender path-gradient ratios from roughly 13,000x to about 5x. Sender relative updates become 10.67x the receiver's and message RMS becomes 3.83x baseline. Yet intact and wrong-world exact match tie in all 50 scheduled evaluations, and the median likelihood gap is only +0.000035 nats. Larger gradients and carrier magnitude are therefore insufficient under the current answer-only objective; this joint treatment also costs 58.11% throughput and cannot isolate capacity from residual initialization.
15. **Approximately fourfold coupled capacity is also ruled out as a sufficient fix.** Scaling the sender, receiver, message-vector width, and output hidden layer together leaves intact no better than wrong-world in exact count over 50 evaluations and produces only a sign-unstable median likelihood gap of +0.000107 nats. The pre-transition loss near 5.303 nats matches the unconditional answer-prior entropy, while the final 3.992-nat training loss matches the 3.971-nat floor available from recognizing the answer domain in the receiver question. Receiver norm growth is concentrated in the vocabulary readout, reinforcing the conclusion that the sharp step-1,020 transition is a domain-prior circuit rather than two-hop communication.

## Current belief

The baseline is definitively in a question-only shortcut/local minimum, not merely weak at communication. Masking the receiver question path makes learned messages necessary, and matched wrong-question controls show that those messages carry question identity, but longer messages and a synchronous sender exchange remain passage-flat. Making the question only the receiver query over message keys and values also fails without extra labels: sender gradients rise modestly, yet aligned passages offer no benefit and raw accuracy drops to 3/256. Directly amplifying sender-private AdamW updates by 4x changes the loss timing and relative sender motion but still yields no aligned-passage advantage over 2,000 or 10,000 steps. A shared receiver-sized sender plus an average-passage residual then removes raw sender-gradient starvation almost completely, drives sender-relative updates far above the receiver's, and makes messages much larger; intact and wrong-world nevertheless tie at all 50 scheduled evaluations. Scaling both sender and receiver to roughly four times their parameter counts produces the same causal failure. Its loss moves from the analytically expected unconditional answer-prior entropy to the question-conditioned domain-prior entropy, while most receiver norm growth sits in the vocabulary readout. The failure is therefore not an initialization-scale diagnosis, too little aggregate sender movement is not the sole blocker, and simply increasing carrier capacity or magnitude is insufficient. Explicit bridge supervision changes the causal result: bridge accuracy reaches 32/256, wrong-question and wrong-passage bridge controls fall to the 6/256 empirical constant baseline, and intact answers beat wrong passages by 5/256 plus 0.0431 nats. Insufficient bridge-selection credit was therefore a material blocker in this setup. Coupled hidden/message width 256 preserves beneficial passage use but does not improve the answer result and costs 26.81% throughput. Tying at width 256 improves raw bridge and answer accuracy slightly plus throughput, but further weakens the passage-specific causal advantage. The best tested scientific arm remains hidden 128, untied, one round, message length 4, receiver question masked, with explicit bridge supervision.

## Evidence limitations

- Existing comparisons are single-seed experiments on the first 256 validation examples.
- The earlier tied-width, role-gate, small-prefix, and one-example runs did not include causal message interventions, so their message use is inferred from output concentration and gradient imbalance rather than directly measured.
- The earlier read-only W&B reconciliation could enumerate projects but not runs, so the earlier values above come from checked-in effective configs, metrics JSONL, outputs JSONL, launcher logs, and reports. All three bridge-supervised runs (hidden-128 untied, hidden-256 untied, and hidden-256 tied), the question-query/message-KV run, its 2,000-step 4x sender-update treatment, and both 10,000-step 1M-question arms were separately verified through the authenticated W&B API as finished at their stated steps with matching final metrics.
